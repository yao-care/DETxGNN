## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action (MOA) data for ketoconazole is not available in this evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, ketoconazole belongs to the **imidazole antifungal class**, and its efficacy in treating fungal infections is well established.

The literature evidence, however, points to a plausible mechanistic bridge to acne: ketoconazole has been shown *in vitro* to inhibit the lipase activity of *Propionibacterium (Cutibacterium) acnes* (PMID 28111792), the bacterium implicated in acne pathogenesis, and other azole antifungals show similar anti-*P. acnes* activity (PMID 20045949). Since sebum-metabolizing lipase activity drives the inflammatory cascade in acne, an agent that suppresses this enzyme — independent of its antifungal action — could plausibly reduce acne severity. This mechanistic rationale is consistent with the drug now being tested clinically as a topical alternative to retinoids for mild acne (NCT07237763).

Several literature entries in this pack (e.g., neonatal *Malassezia*/*Pityrosporum* folliculitis misdiagnosed as acne) further illustrate ketoconazole's established role in treating fungal-driven pustular skin conditions that clinically overlap with acne presentations, lending indirect support to repurposing.

A blocking data gap exists — TFDA/BfArM label warnings and contraindications are unavailable, which prevents completion of the S1 safety pre-assessment. Clinical evidence is also limited to a single ongoing Phase NA trial, with most supporting literature being *in vitro* or mechanistic rather than confirmatory clinical studies.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Obtain official package insert / label data (warnings, contraindications) from TFDA or BfArM
